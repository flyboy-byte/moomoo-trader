# Systematic Intraday Paper Trading — Four-Track Research Report

## TL;DR
- **Track 1 is the big unlock and is mostly confirmed in Moomoo's own docs:** extended-hours candles (`extended_time=True`), 60-level US order book (now free), tick-by-tick with side flags, full options chains with greeks/IV/OI for SPY/QQQ/IWM, and CME index-futures quotes all exist in the API — but extended-hours history "may be less than 2 years," and US futures/options *trading* is not available to Moomoo US accounts (quote access is separate).
- **The single highest-ROI, lowest-cost action is to wire in Moomoo extended-hours 1-/5-min candles to compute a pre-market gap-fill % and pre-market volume tier for the Gap Fade strategy** — the literature strongly supports that small "common" gaps (<0.5× ATR) fill 70–80% of the time, that low pre-market volume is the best "noise gap" discriminator, and that IWM's small-cap noise profile explains its 72% OOS win rate.
- **Options-flow/GEX is real and orthogonal but should wait:** the evidence for positive-gamma vol suppression and OpEx pinning is solid, but at 10–50 live trades it is premature; a free/cheap minimal GEX regime tag built from Moomoo's own options chain is the right first step, not a SpotGamma/SqueezeMetrics subscription.

## Key Findings

1. **Pre-market data is already in your API.** Moomoo's `request_history_kline(..., extended_time=True)` and `subscribe(..., session=Session.ALL)` return pre-market/after-hours candles for US stocks/ETFs at 60-min-and-below timeframes. Implementation is days, not months. The only real constraint is retention: extended-hours history "may be less than 2 years."
2. **The Gap Fade strategy is the best-supported improvement target.** Academic and practitioner evidence converges: small/common gaps fade reliably; pre-market volume (not gap size alone) is the key informational-vs-noise discriminator; small caps (IWM) are noisier and mean-revert more, explaining the IWM>SPY>QQQ ordering you already observe.
3. **Futures premium predictiveness is weakly supported.** There is practitioner-backtest evidence (not peer-reviewed) that an overnight move *against* the gap raises fill odds, but samples are small and the dominant predictors are gap size and opening location.
4. **GEX mechanics are well-established; 0DTE net impact is contested and small.** Positive GEX = vol suppression/mean-reversion regime; negative GEX = amplification/trend regime. But CBOE finds 0DTE *net* dealer gamma hedging flows make up only 0.04–0.17% of daily S&P futures liquidity with "no discernible market impact," and recent academic work finds 0DTEs, if anything, dampen vol.
5. **Data-vendor sweet spot for ≤$100/mo:** you likely do not need to spend anything. Moomoo extended-hours candles (free) + Moomoo options chain (free) cover both primary needs. If you want clean historical pre-market backfill beyond Moomoo's <2-year window, FirstRate Data one-time bundles or Databento metered pulls (covered by free credits) are the cheapest top-ups.

**Q8 — WebSocket push latency vs polling.** *Confirmed mechanism.* Handler base classes (`TickerHandlerBase`, `OrderBookHandlerBase`, plus K-line/quote handlers) deliver server pushes asynchronously — these are genuine real-time tick/quote/orderbook pushes, not just candle pushes. ### TRACK 2 — Pre-Market Gap Characterization

**Q1 — ETF gap fill rates; small vs large.** Strong consensus that **small/"common" gaps fill far more than large ones.** Per JournalPlus, citing Bulkowski's *Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns*: "Common gaps are small — under 0.5x the 14-day ATR... These fill the same session 70–80% of the time," whereas breakaway gaps ">1x ATR... 2–3x the 30-day average [volume]... continue... roughly 55–65% of the time." Bulkowski's broader dataset (thepatternsite.com, 520 stocks, 1990–2016) finds "at least half the gap fills 84% of the time" across all gap sizes — but with an important regime shift: same-day *full* closure fell from "48%... in the 1990s" to "just 23%... in the 2010s," so do not assume full intraday fills. Practitioner futures backtests agree on the size gradient: a 2,791-day NQ study reports a ~60% baseline full-fill rate with tiny gaps filling ~78% and large (>1.2× ATR) gaps only ~8%. Your 0.3–2% Gap Fade band sits squarely in the "common/noise" regime where fades have empirical support; most fills happen well before your 11:00 ET time stop.

**Q2 — Futures premium at the open as a predictor.** *Weakly supported, practitioner-grade only.* A TradingStats NQ study found "Gap down but overnight rallied: 83.3% fill rate" — i.e., when overnight futures move *against* the gap (already fading it), fill odds jump — but on only 30 cases. The same source reports the RTH open relative to the overnight midpoint predicts session direction with ~76% accuracy, and that "one in three NQ gaps fill within the opening 5-minute bar … because the RTH open often reverses the overnight gap as institutional order flow kicks in." **No peer-reviewed study isolates this exact signal**, and SharePlanner cautions Monday/Sunday-night futures signals are less reliable. **Verdict: treat ES/NQ pre-open direction as a secondary confirmation, not a primary filter; the dominant predictors remain gap size and opening location (inside vs outside prior day's range: ~70% vs ~43–47% fill).**

**Q3 — Pre-market partial fill effect.** No clean academic answer, but the practitioner consensus cuts toward your second hypothesis with a caveat: a gap **already being faded in pre-market on rising volume confirms fade pressure** (the 83.3% "overnight rallied" datapoint supports this). However, practitioners warn explicitly that "a partial fill that reverses is a known trap" [JournalPlus](https://journalplus.co/learn/glossary/gap-fill/) — so a 50–70% pre-market fill *that then stalls/reverses before 9:30* is a warning, not a green light. Recommended encoding: compute pre-market fill % at ~9:20–9:29; **a partial fill still progressing toward prior close = stronger signal; a partial fill that has stalled/reversed = stand aside.**

**Q4 — Informational vs noise gap classification.** The best single discriminator in the literature is **pre-market volume relative to average**, not gap size. JournalPlus states it directly: "High pre-market volume on a gap-up signals institutional participation — a warning sign for any fade. Low pre-market volume on a small gap is a green light for the common-gap-fade setup." Quantified rule of thumb for SPY: pre-market volume <20M = low, 20–40M = medium, 40M+ (≈1.5–2× the ~28M 30-day average) = high/institutional-conviction. [JournalPlus](https://journalplus.co/strategies/opening-gap-strategy/) Common (fadeable) gaps form on low relative volume with no catalyst; breakaway (do-not-fade) gaps form on 2–3× average pre-market volume with an identifiable catalyst and continue 55–65% of the time. Add a calendar/catalyst check (earnings, CPI, FOMC) as a hard veto.

**Q5 — Small-cap vs large-cap gap behavior (why IWM wins).** Multiple strands explain IWM (72% OOS) > SPY (60%) > QQQ (~flat): (a) Small-cap indices have a higher proportion of retail/noise-driven overnight order flow; the overnight-return/daytime-reversal literature (the *Journal of Financial Economics* "tug of war" work, Lou/Polk-style and successors) shows overnight moves driven by uninformed/retail demand get reverted intraday by daytime arbitrageurs. (b) Small caps have less single-name, news-driven overnight repricing than QQQ (which is dominated by mega-cap tech with idiosyncratic, *informational* overnight moves — exactly the gaps that should NOT fade, explaining QQQ's flat PF). (c) SPY sits between. The mechanism is structural: IWM gaps are more often liquidity/noise gaps; QQQ gaps more often informational. This is consistent with your data and argues for **keeping IWM as the primary Gap Fade symbol and being much more selective (or excluding) QQQ.**

**Q6 — Pre-market volume threshold with empirical support.** The most-cited actionable thresholds: for index ETFs, **pre-market volume below ~1× the 20–30-day pre-market average (and especially a small gap on thin volume) is the fade green light**; above ~1.5–2× average flags institutional conviction and should veto the fade. For SPY specifically, the 40M+ pre-market-share tier (~1.5–2× the ~28M average) is the documented conviction cutoff. [JournalPlus](https://journalplus.co/strategies/opening-gap-strategy/) Since IWM is your best symbol, **calibrate an IWM-specific pre-market volume baseline** (Moomoo extended-hours candles give you exactly this) rather than reusing SPY numbers.

### TRACK 3 — Options Flow as Intraday Regime Filter

**Q1 — GEX mechanics.** GEX = the dollar value of underlying that options dealers must buy/sell per unit move to stay delta-neutral, aggregated as Σ(gamma × OI × contract multiplier × spot, signed by dealer position) across strikes/expiries. **Positive GEX** (dealers net long gamma): dealers buy dips/sell rips → volatility suppression, range-bound "grind," mean-reversion regime. **Negative GEX** (dealers net short gamma): dealers sell weakness/buy strength → amplification, trending/violent regime (the Aug 5 2024 yen-carry unwind is the canonical negative-GEX example). The price where net gamma flips sign is the "gamma flip"/"zero-gamma" level. **Pinning** evidence is academically solid: Ni, Pearson & Poteshman (2005, *Journal of Financial Economics* 78:49–87) show optionable-stock closing prices cluster at strikes on expiration, [Illinois Experts](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/stock-price-clustering-on-option-expiration-dates/) altering returns by ≥16.5 bp and shifting ~$9B in market cap, attributed to dealer hedge rebalancing. [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=519044) Follow-on work (Pearson, Poteshman & White) finds ~12% of daily absolute returns of optioned stocks attributable to hedge rebalancing — not just on expiration. [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/101221759/Does_Option_Trading_Have_a_Pervasive_Impact_on_Underlying_Stock_Prices) **Caveat:** OI is an end-of-day lagging snapshot; positive gamma is "associated with stability, not deterministic pinning."

**Q2 — 0DTE flow lead/lag.** *Evidence quality: mixed-to-good, and it cuts AGAINST the "0DTE destabilizes" narrative.* CBOE's research ("Evaluating the Market Impact of SPX 0DTE Options") finds 0DTE customer flow is balanced between puts/calls, with "average net gamma exposure ranging from $170mm... potential hedging flows that make up between 0.04% to 0.17% of the daily S&P futures liquidity. There is also no discernible market impact from 0DTE option trading, with SPX index intraday volatility and price patterns in line with historical averages." This matters because 0DTE is now the majority of SPX volume (CBOE via IBKR: "rising from 5% of SPX options volume in 2016 to over 40%... averaging 50% in August"; by 2025 SPX 0DTE "averaged about 2.3 million contracts a day and made up 59 per cent of SPX volume"). A recent academic paper (Adams, Dim, Eraker, Fontaine, Ornthanalai, Vilkov; SSRN 2024–25) argues 0DTEs *dampen* SPX volatility: "variations in market makers' net gamma predict lower S&P500 volatility in the next 10 minutes, and this effect dissipates after about one hour." [QuantPedia](https://quantpedia.com/do-sp500-0dtes-options-increase-market-volatility/) So any predictive signal is **short-horizon (≤~1 hour) and regime/vol-related, not directional.**

**Q3 — OpEx calendar effects.** Documented and tradeable. OpEx mornings (monthly third Friday) tend to be vol-compressed/range-bound (mean-reversion-favorable, anchored near max pain), with a "gamma cliff" the following Monday flipping toward a more directional/trend regime as the largest OI rolls off. Quantified backtest: SPY's single OPEX-day return (close-to-close) is historically poor (compounded −23.55%, 51.4% win in one SPY backtest) — i.e., not a directional edge, but the **intraday character (compress-then-afternoon-trend) is the exploitable part.** Implication for your book: **BB+KDJ and VWAP-PB mean-reversion should fare relatively better on OpEx mornings; ORB breakout should fare relatively worse on OpEx mornings and better the Monday after.**

**Q4 — Gamma pinning near strikes (0DTE).** Ni–Pearson–Poteshman establishes the monthly-expiry pinning effect; a follow-up (William Paterson Univ.) finds weekly-option expirations show a *weaker* clustering effect than monthlies ("expiration of weekly options does not have the same impact as … monthly options"). So **0DTE/weekly pinning is real but materially weaker than monthly-OpEx pinning.** For your strategies: on high-OI monthly-expiry days, price gravitating toward the dominant strike would **suppress ORB follow-through and VWAP-reclaim continuation** near that strike — a reason to down-weight breakout signals into a nearby high-OI strike.

**Q6 — Practical recommendation.** **Wait on paid options data.** At 10–50 live trades you cannot yet attribute edge to a regime filter without overfitting. The correct minimal step is a **free, self-computed daily GEX/regime tag**: pull SPY/QQQ/IWM option snapshots via `get_market_snapshot`, aggregate Σ(gamma×OI) per strike, estimate a net sign and the dominant strike, and **log it as metadata on every trade** (do not gate entries yet). After ~100+ trades, test whether mean-reversion strategies outperform in positive-GEX/low-vol tags and ORB outperforms in negative-GEX tags. A minimal viable filter, once validated, would be: *suppress ORB when net GEX is strongly positive and price is within ~0.3% of the dominant monthly strike; allow/upsize mean-reversion in the same regime.*
