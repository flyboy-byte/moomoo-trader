## Moomoo API capabilities that matter right now

The most important Track 1 answer is yes: Moomoo documents historical U.S. extended-hours candles. The method is `request_history_kline(code, start=None, end=None, ktype=..., autype=..., fields=..., max_count=..., page_req_key=None, extended_time=False)`. The docs explicitly state that `extended_time=True` requests premarket and after-hours data for U.S. stocks, and that the `session` parameter can be used for U.S. historical k-line retrieval. The same page states that U.S. premarket, after-hours, and overnight candlesticks are supported only for 60-minute-and-below timeframes, and warns that irregular-hours candle history may be less than two years. For your 5-minute ETF workflow, that is the right granularity. citeturn14view0turn14view1turn15view0

The boundary definitions are also documented. Moomoo’s quote Q&A page defines U.S. securities premarket as 04:00 to 09:30 Eastern, regular trading as 09:30 to 16:00 Eastern, after-hours as 16:00 to 20:00 Eastern, and the overnight closed period as 20:00 to 04:00 Eastern. That means your current “overnight gap” estimate can be replaced with something much more precise: prior regular close, overnight closed-session change, premarket path, premarket high and low, and the residual gap still standing at 09:29:59. citeturn22view0

For live state monitoring, the real-time quote callback is richer than many people realize. The `StockQuoteHandlerBase` callback page shows fields not only for core quote state such as `last_price`, `open_price`, `high_price`, `low_price`, `volume`, and `turnover`, but also dedicated premarket fields like `pre_price`, `pre_high_price`, `pre_low_price`, `pre_volume`, `pre_turnover`, `pre_change_val`, `pre_change_rate`, and `pre_amplitude`; after-hours fields like `after_price`, `after_high_price`, `after_low_price`, `after_volume`, `after_turnover`, `after_change_val`, `after_change_rate`, and `after_amplitude`; plus overnight fields such as `overnight_price`, `overnight_high_price`, `overnight_low_price`, `overnight_volume`, `overnight_turnover`, `overnight_change_val`, `overnight_change_rate`, and `overnight_amplitude`. That is enough to start building a live premarket classifier without leaving the Moomoo ecosystem. citeturn24view0

On depth and microstructure, the answer is also mostly yes, with caveats. `get_order_book(code, num=10, order_book_type=None)` is documented and returns bid and ask ladders. Moomoo’s quote FAQ states U.S. stocks support 60 Nasdaq levels plus 60 Arca levels under its depth-of-book integration, and that U.S. futures support 40 levels. `get_broker_queue(code)` is also documented, but its broker-ID and order-ID richness is largely Hong Kong-specific; the page repeatedly notes that some queue-detail fields are only supported for HK SF quotes. So for your U.S. ETF use case, order book is meaningful; broker queue is documented, but likely not where the edge is. citeturn12view0turn15view2turn22view0

On tick and time-and-sales, the answer is yes for real time and unclear for historical retention. `get_rt_ticker(code, num=500)` is documented as real-time tick-by-tick and returns timestamp, trade price, volume, turnover, direction, and type. `get_rt_data(code)` is documented as real-time time-frame data and returns per-minute-style records including current price, last close, average price, volume, and turnover. What I did **not** find in the current documented quote pages I reviewed was a clearly documented historical tick-by-tick endpoint comparable to `request_history_kline`. So the high-confidence finding is that Moomoo exposes live tick-by-tick, but I would not assume documented historical tick depth or retention until you validate it directly in your build or in a smaller doc pass focused only on that question. citeturn15view3turn13view0turn12view3

On options, Moomoo’s documented surface is broader than your current stack uses. `get_option_expiration_date(...)` returns expiries; `get_option_chain(...)` returns static chain information and can filter on implied volatility, delta, gamma, vega, theta, rho, open interest, and volume; `get_option_volatility(...)` returns implied volatility, historical volatility, volatility premium, average implied volatility, and an analysis field for a specified option contract; and `get_option_quote(...)` returns snapshot-style quote and Greeks data for option strategy legs. That is enough to support basic regime tagging and per-strike gravity heuristics, although not necessarily a clean institutional-grade GEX engine by itself. citeturn19view0turn17view0turn19view2turn18view0

On futures, the docs clearly state that Moomoo supports futures in both quote coverage and trading capacity tables, and `get_future_info(code_list)` is documented for futures contract information. The introductory docs explicitly say the universal account supports futures including U.S. CME Group futures. The key limitation is that in this pass I did not verify the exact symbol mapping for ES, NQ, and RTY inside the current Python/OpenD namespace. ## What premarket research implies for your gap and open logic

The opening problem you described is real in market-microstructure terms. The opening auction and pre-open period are not just dead time before “real trading.” They are part of the market’s price-discovery process. Nasdaq’s opening-cross logic exists precisely because market-moving information accumulates outside regular hours, and pre-open imbalances help determine the opening print. Academic work on U.S. opening and closing auctions also finds structured, non-random behavior in pre-auction imbalance and indicative price dynamics. That means a strategy that measures a “gap” only as yesterday’s close versus the first regular-session bar is necessarily collapsing materially different states into one number. citeturn28news0turn35academia0

There is also relevant evidence that overnight and intraday returns are behaviorally and statistically different objects, not interchangeable segments of one simple daily return. Work decomposing overnight and intraday effects finds that they behave differently, and newer research ties a meaningful share of overnight return behavior to overnight news. That matters for you because “informational” gaps and “liquidity/noise” gaps should not be expected to fade the same way. A gap around earnings, CPI, FOMC, or a genuine macro shock is not just a larger version of a sleepy overnight dislocation. citeturn27academia0turn33academia0turn33news2

That point becomes even stronger when you zoom in on explicit information events. The long literature around post-earnings-announcement drift is one of the clearest examples that information shocks can continue in the direction of the news after the initial move instead of mean-reverting immediately. I would not mechanically map PEAD onto ETF gap fades, but the principle is load-bearing: when the overnight move is genuinely information-rich, “fade the gap” becomes less attractive and “respect the information” becomes more attractive. citeturn33search3turn33academia0

The practical implication is that premarket candles are very likely to improve your gap-fade strategy materially, but the most useful signal is not “premarket exists.” It is the **state of the residual gap at the open**. In your language, the feature you want is not raw overnight gap size, but something like:

`remaining_gap_pct_at_0929 = (09:29 reference price - prior_regular_close) / (09:30 official open - prior_regular_close)`

or the equivalent residual-versus-original ratio. If the overnight move has already been largely repaired before the bell, the opening regular-hours trade is a very different setup than a fresh, intact dislocation still standing at 09:29. That conclusion is an inference from opening price-discovery evidence, not a directly estimated “70%” law from the literature I reviewed; but it is a well-supported inference and a much stronger feature than your current blind spot. citeturn22view0turn28news0turn35academia0

I did **not** find a clean peer-reviewed paper that gives a universal premarket-volume cutoff or a universal “skip if X% filled” threshold for U.S. ETF gap fades. So I would not treat any single threshold as “research proved.” What the evidence does support is directional rather than exact: more premarket price discovery, more imbalance adjustment before the bell, and more identifiable news content all argue for conditioning the gap-fade signal on premarket path, residual gap, and event context rather than gap size alone. citeturn35academia0turn33academia0

On ES/NQ futures premium right before the cash open, my conclusion is cautious. Futures clearly participate in pre-open price discovery and are widely used to estimate the implied open. That gives economic logic to including ES or NQ basis at 09:29 as a feature. But in this pass I did not find high-confidence ETF-specific evidence showing that a simple 09:29 futures premium by itself is a robust standalone predictor of same-day gap-fade success. So I would use it as a feature in a classifier or post-trade analysis, not as a hard gate on day one. citeturn31news0turn31search1

For IWM specifically, the most honest answer is that I found a plausible mechanism, not a settled proof. Research on order-book and limit-order dynamics across market-cap tiers shows lower market-cap names tend to have lower stability and different opening behavior, and small-cap structures are generally more liquidity-fragile than large-cap structures. It is therefore plausible that a diversified small-cap ETF can display stronger short-horizon mean reversion after opening dislocations because constituent-level idiosyncratic noise compresses into ETF-level dislocation that can partially wash out. But I did **not** find a direct paper saying “IWM gaps fade better than SPY because of X.” Treat that as a working theory rather than a fact. citeturn34academia7turn34academia3

## What options-flow research says and what it does not say

GEX, in practical terms, is a dealer-positioning proxy built from option gamma across strikes and expiries. The intuition is straightforward: if dealers are structurally long gamma around spot, their hedging flows tend to damp price moves and pull price back toward local strikes; if they are short gamma, their hedging can reinforce moves and make breaks run farther. That mechanic is economically coherent and is exactly why strike “pinning” and gamma-regime language have become common in practitioner workflows. The problem is not that the idea is crazy. The problem is that the amount of hard, clean, public evidence behind simple retail-style GEX dashboards is much smaller than the amount of marketing and social-media certainty around them. citeturn42search7turn41academia0

The rise of 0DTE options is unquestionably real. Cboe-linked reporting in 2025 showed 0DTE contracts accounting for roughly 60% or more of S&P 500-linked options volume, with average daily volume around 2 million contracts or more depending on the period reported. So if you are asking whether 0DTE flow has become large enough to plausibly matter to intraday index behavior, the answer is yes. The harder question is whether off-the-shelf 0DTE flow metrics available to a small trader reliably improve a three-strategy ETF paper system at your current scale. On that question, this pass does not support a strong “yes.” citeturn39news1turn39news3turn39news4

OpEx effects land in the same bucket. The phenomenon of higher volume and occasional volatility around expiration is real and longstanding, especially in “triple witching” settings. But the existence of an expiration-day effect is not the same as having a tradable, stable, simple rule that says “mean reversion is better on OpEx” or “breakouts are worse on OpEx” for SPY, QQQ, and IWM on your horizons. The literature and reference material support caution and conditioning, not a naive calendar-on/calendar-off switch. citeturn42search1turn42search7

That leads to the practical recommendation. At your current stage, options data should be used, if at all, as a **regime label**, not as a new alpha engine. The best low-complexity use would be daily tags such as whether spot is very near the dominant nearest-term strike cluster, whether the day is major expiration, whether implied volatility is compressed or elevated relative to recent history, and whether the underlying is likely in a pinning-friendly environment. Moomoo’s own option endpoints are enough to experiment with chain structure, IV, open interest, and Greeks without buying a full third-party GEX product immediately. citeturn17view0turn19view2turn18view0

My recommendation is therefore negative rather than positive: **do not make paid real-time GEX your first external data purchase**. The mechanical idea is real, but for a system still early in live-paper evidence accumulation, premarket characterization has a much cleaner causal link to your current edge case, lower integration risk, and better fit with the strongest strategy opportunity you described. citeturn22view0turn33academia0turn35academia0

Its site states that ETF historical intraday bundles include 1-minute through daily data from January 2000 through June 2026 for 5,162 ETFs, with out-of-hours trades included, and updates priced at $59.95 per month after the included first month. ## Recommended implementation path

The first build step should be to add a dedicated premarket and opening-state layer using Moomoo alone. Pull 5-minute historical U.S. k-lines with `extended_time=True`, and store at least these derived features for every trading day: previous regular close, 04:00-to-09:29 return, premarket high and low, premarket volume, percent of original gap already retraced before 09:30, whether premarket traded back through the prior close, and whether the first regular-session print opens outside the premarket range. That directly addresses the biggest structural blind spot in the current gap-fade logic. citeturn14view0turn15view0turn22view0

The second step should be to turn the live quote callback into a richer session monitor. Because Moomoo already exposes `pre_*`, `after_*`, and `overnight_*` fields in the real-time quote callback, you can log live session-state snapshots without inventing a separate reference feed. That gives you the ability to compare replay, historical, and live-paper behavior using one native broker data source first. citeturn24view0

The third step should be to add a single outside validation source, not a new real-time dependency web. If you want to audit Moomoo’s 5-minute data quality or build a reference check for the most important symbols, FirstRate looks like the most clearly documented sub-$100-ish path from the sources verified here, especially because it includes out-of-hours ETF data and a historical tick/quote bundle. The right use case is not “replace Moomoo immediately.” It is “sample-check Moomoo on event days, opening bars, and extended-hours transitions.” citeturn59view1turn59view0turn59view2

The fourth step should be to keep options-flow work deliberately small. Use daily regime tags before you buy a live GEX product. Examples include nearest dominant strike cluster, rough distance from spot to high-open-interest strikes, expiry-day tag, and simple IV state. Moomoo’s option chain and option-volatility endpoints are enough to prototype this cheaply. citeturn17view0turn19view2

If I collapse everything into one sentence: the best next dollar to spend is probably **zero dollars**, because the best next feature is very likely already exposed by Moomoo. citeturn14view0turn24view0turn22view0
